Agentic AI and biostatistics: one-way or two-way street? And how? Sharing [the deck](https://lnkd.in/gA8RCgRx) used in our lab discussion today.



`Context`: AI that plans, calls tools, and loops until a task is done is becoming more real. This week alone, Nature published “the anatomy of personal health agent”: a system that writes statistical analysis plans and code over wearable data, evaluated with 7,000+ human annotations.

Prompted by this, I asked my biostatistics group 4 uncomfortable questions:

→ What is one task you would hand to an agent from the next week/month? What is the one you’d never hand over? Is that because of quality, trust, or because “the struggle is where you learn”?

→ Which of the methods we already work on become more valuable in a space where AI agents are used routinely, and which become less?

→ If an agent ran the main analysis in our next paper, what would we need to log, disclose, and be able to re-run before we would put our names on it?

→ Would you let an agent interpret your own sleep and glucose data? Would you let it interpret a study participant’s? Implications for new health information sharing, interpretation, and population studies?

`My take`: for statisticians, agents are not a threat to the field but a stress test of it. Uncertainty quantification for model outputs, evaluation design, and causal reasoning about sequential decisions just became more valuable, not less. Especially in low-resource settings, where data may be sparse and where these systems are least tested and statistical wisdom may help.

The deck includes a reading list covering `foundations`, `agents-as-scientists`, and `critiques`.
